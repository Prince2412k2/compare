The Breakdown

Pros & Cons

Kodak FunSaver

ISO 800 · The Nostalgic Workhorse
Pros
  • ISO 800 means dramatically better low-light and indoor flash results
  • Iconic warm, saturated palette — instantly recognisable look
  • Very forgiving in mixed lighting; rarely blows highlights badly
  • 39-exposure version available for more shots per roll
  • Widest availability globally — any pharmacy or supermarket
  • Punchy skin tones with golden warmth that flatters people
  • Best flash performance of the three for parties and nightlife
Cons
  • ISO 800 = noticeable grain, especially in bright daylight
  • Colours can read as overly warm / orange to some eyes
  • Flash coverage drops off quickly beyond ~3 metres
  • Lens softness noticeable at the corners of the frame
  • Not ideal if you want accurate, neutral colour reproduction

Fuji QuickSnap

ISO 400 · The Precision Choice
Pros
  • Most accurate, neutral colour rendering — greens and blues exceptional
  • Finer grain at ISO 400 gives cleaner, sharper-looking scans
  • Superb for outdoor daylight — this is its natural habitat
  • Skin tones are natural and rarely over-saturated
  • Consistent and predictable roll to roll — excellent reliability
  • Faster shutter (1/140s) helps freeze action more effectively
  • Processed easily at virtually every lab worldwide
Cons
  • ISO 400 struggles more indoors compared to the FunSaver
  • Cool colour profile can feel clinical or flat in some scenes
  • Less "character" — results can look almost digital
  • Only 27 exposures — no extended version available
  • Neutral palette can make scenes feel less dramatic

Agfa Le Box 400

ISO 400 · The Artistic Wildcard
Pros
  • Distinctive muted, washed-out aesthetic hugely popular right now
  • Beautiful organic grain — the most genuinely filmic of the three
  • Often cheapest option — great for experimentation on a budget
  • Lifted shadows and faded look gives shots a dreamy editorial feel
  • Excellent for creative shoots where perfection isn't the priority
  • Happy accidents — surprises delight as much as they disappoint
Cons
  • Least consistent of the three — results vary roll to roll
  • Harder to find; often online-only in many countries
  • Low contrast can produce flat, muddy results in poor light
  • Flash results less flattering than Kodak or Fuji
  • Lab scanning choices affect outcome more than the other two
  • Not a safe choice for events needing reliable portraits

The Final Verdict

Best for parties & low light
Kodak FunSaver

Shooting indoors, at night, or just want that warm punchy film look with maximum reliability? ISO 800 is an unbeatable superpower.

Best all-rounder
Fuji QuickSnap

Travel, landscapes, events — wherever you need accurate, clean, and trustworthy results, the QuickSnap delivers the most technically sound photos.

Best for aesthetics & art
Agfa Le Box 400

Love that washed-out, dreamy European film look and don't need perfection? The Le Box is budget-friendly, characterful, and genuinely surprising.

Our recommendation: buy all three, load them at the same event, and compare. The best disposable camera is the one that matches the story you're trying to tell.
